ABSTRACT
I have been using TrueShield AI for a few months. It feels like a real guardian for my
phone. TrueShield AI is a on‑device system that uses two engines to spot and stop
scam calls while they happen. It is built to keep phone users safe from voice
phishing, financial tricks and fake identity theft. With fraud growing through VoIP
spoofing, SIM‑box farming and synthetic AI voices old methods like crowd‑sourced
caller‑ID lists and number blacklists are not enough. They check the caller only after
the scam is done and do not see what is being said in the call. TrueShield AI uses a
dual‑engine design. One part is a speech‑to‑text tool called Automatic Speech
Recognition. The other part is a pattern matcher that looks for known scam words.
Both parts work together with a deep understanding engine powered by Google
Gemini 1.5 Flash. TrueShield AI only works with the user’s permission. It records the
call on the phone keeps it in a short‑term memory and turns the speech into text
instantly. TrueShield AI sends the speech to two paths. Path one uses a matcher that
searches a list of more than 95 scam words in English, Hindi and Hinglish. These
words are chosen for phone scams like stolen OTPs fake KYC stops, power cut
notices and remote‑control Trojan attacks such as AnyDesk. Path two uses a
language model that looks at how the caller talks whether they are using pressure,
urgency or pretending to be an official. Both paths send their findings to a risk
calculator that picks the danger level. A 6‑second safety timer stops the system if
something goes wrong. The result is a risk score from 0 to 100. Trueshield AI shows a
clear warning on a small non‑intrusive screen, on the phone. We tested TrueShield
AI on 240 conversations. It found scams 92.4% of the time was 94.7% of the time
and caught 93.6% of all real scams. The whole process takes than 1.9 seconds so the
user sees a warning almost instantly. TrueShield AI keeps the user’s privacy safe.

1.1 Background and Motivation
Nowadays smartphones and digital platforms are essential for communication. People
use their phones to talk do banking and make online payments. With this growing use
of technology there has also been a rise in cybercrimes. One of the common forms is
vishing.
Vishing is short for voice phishing. It happens when fraudsters pretend to be someone
like a bank officer, telecom employee or customer service agent. They use phone calls
to trick people into giving out information such as passwords, OTPs or account details.
These scams are hard to catch because they use caller ID spoofing, VoIP services and
even artificial intelligence tools to sound real.
Most spam filters today work by blocking known numbers or checking caller
reputation.. These methods have flaws. Scammers can easily switch phone numbers.
Create fake identities. Also the filters only look at the number not what is actually said
in the call. So the conversation itself goes unnoticed.
Proposed Project TrueShield AI
The project is called "TrueShield AI: An AI-Based Scam Call Guardian." This name
reflects the use of intelligence in detecting scam calls. The system uses speech-based
technologies like Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Natural Language
Processing (NLP). It listens to the conversation converts speech into text and analyzes
the meaning behind the words.
It looks for signs of fraud—like pressure to act fast identity claims or requests for
sensitive data. It also uses Explainable AI (XAI) to show users why a call is flagged as
suspicious. This helps people understand the risk without needing knowledge.
1.2 Problem Statement
Vishing attacks are getting smarter and harder to catch. Scammers use tools like
spoofed caller IDs, VoIP networks and convincing scripts to trick users. They often
target details like one-time passwords, bank login info or KYC data.
11

PDF PAGE 12
Current solutions for blocking scam calls rely on lists of known spam numbers or user
reports. These systems are not fast enough. Cannot adapt to new attack patterns.
They are also limited because they only look at numbers not at the actual
conversation content.
Many machine learning models depend on keyword detection.. Scammers can easily
change their words or use new phrases. This makes such tools ineffective. Also delays
in detection can be costly—by the time a scam is recognized damage may already be
done.
Another problem is privacy. Many existing tools collect amounts of data including call
audio. This raises concerns about how personal information's used and stored. There
is a need for a system that protects users without invading their privacy.
The solution lies in time AI-powered analysis of conversations. It should be able to
understand the intent behind the words spoken in a call and detect fraud instantly.
1.3 Objectives of the Project
1. To. Implement a real-time client-side system that combines streaming Automatic
Speech Recognition (ASR) with a fast lexical analyzer (under 5ms) and uses Google
Gemini 1.5 Flash LLM for semantic understanding.
2. To build a domain-specific dictionary of over 95 fraud-related terms in English,
Hindi and Hinglish. These terms will reflect scam patterns seen in India, such as UPI
fraud, OTP theft, KYC scams and fake electricity bill notices.

watchdog circuit breaker. This ensures detection and avoids delays caused by

4. To build an AI (XAI) interface that gives clear plain-language alerts. It will explain
why a call is risky and suggest actions to take. It will not store audio recordings.
1.4 Scope of the Project
This project focuses on detecting and stopping vishing attacks in time using artificial
intelligence. The system will work on the user’s device with the permission of the
user. It listens to phone conversations converts speech into text and checks for signs
of fraud.
Key components of the project include:
• Real-time speech recognition and transcription
• AI-based analysis of language and meaning ( keyword analysis)
• Dynamic risk scoring based on conversation content
• Explainable AI warning system that gives clear reasons, for alerts without storing
audio data

2.1 Introduction
Fast growth in communication technology has changed how people talk, do business
and use online services.
Advances in smartphones, VoIP, digital banking and cloud communication have made
talking easier and more convenient.
However these advances also make people more vulnerable to kinds of cyber attacks,
such as fraud and telecom tricks.
One of the dangerous attacks is voice phishing, also called vishing, which tricks a
Victim by using voice calls.
Unlike email phishing, which sends emails voice phishing uses real phone calls where
scammers pretend to be people you trust.
Fraudsters often pretend to be bank bosses, customer support staff, government
workers or police officers to gain a victim’s trust.
These attacks use fear, panic and fake urgency to get users to give passwords, OTPs
and private financial details.
As voices caller‑ID spoofing and auto‑calling tools grow fast fake calls get more
clever.
Old spam filters mainly use fixed number blacklists and user reports. They cannot
spot spoofed or changing numbers.
New advances in AI, ASR, NLP and XAI let us create call‑defense tools that can write
down live speech read the meaning of a conversation and spot fraud right away
This chapter reviews research on voice phishing ways to fight telephone fraud,
speech recognition systems NLP models, large language models, deepfake voice
dangers and explainable AI, in security.

2.3 Traditional Spam Detection Systems
Conventional telecommunication spam filters primarily inspect telephone numbers
rather than the actual words spoken during a call. Early commercial tools relied on
caller reputation lists, static blacklists, crowdsourced user reports, and call metadata.
These systems measured network-level traits such as how many times a number calls,
16

how long calls last on average, call origin, and cumulative complaint counts to decide
whether an incoming call is spam. When machine learning entered the scene,
automated classifiers appeared using algorithms like Naïve Bayes, Support Vector
Machines, Decision Trees, Random Forests, and Logistic Regression. Although these
models improved detection for known repeat offenders, they still depended heavily
on historical databases and past calling patterns, which fundamentally limits their
protection.
A notable example comes from the paper "An Ensemble Learning Approach For Spam
Call Detection In Mobile Communication Networks" published in the International
Journal of Research Publication and Reviews in 2024. This study used AdaBoost and
Random Forest classifiers on subscriber call records. The system worked well on
historical call logs, but it only analyzed metadata and could not judge the caller’s
intent or the evolving tricks used during the conversation. Today, when fraudsters
constantly purchase new VoIP lines or spoof legitimate bank numbers, models relying
strictly on metadata fail completely.
Another study, titled "Spam Call Detection Using NLP and Naïve Bayes Classifier",
analyzed speech transcripts after calls concluded. While Naïve Bayes achieved
baseline accuracy on static test sets, it suffers from a major mathematical flaw: it
assumes all words are completely independent. Real human speech relies on
grammar, conversational context, and subtle psychological pressure that cannot be
captured by treating words independently.

A comprehensive literature review requires categorizing the psychological manipulation
vectors exploited by telephonic fraudsters. Social engineering attacks over
telecommunication networks exploit cognitive biases rather than technical software
bugs. Researchers categorize modern phone scams into several distinct attack vectors:
1. Authority and Regulatory Impersonation: Fraudsters pose as representatives of
legitimate government bodies, law enforcement agencies (such as state cyber
crime cells or the Central Bureau of Investigation), or the Reserve Bank of India
(RBI). Attackers cite fictitious legal notices, money-laundering allegations, or non-
existent court warrants to intimidate victims into compliance.
2. Pretexting and Manufactured Urgency: The attacker creates a fabricated scenario

imposing extreme time constraints, scammers prevent victims from verifying
claims through official channels.
3. Credential Harvesting (Vishing): The primary objective of most banking scams is
obtaining two-factor authentication tokens. Scammers use convincing
conversational pretexts (such as refund processing or security server
synchronization) to persuade users to read out one-time passwords (OTPs), card
verification values (CVVs), or UPI authorization PINs.

3.1 Introduction
Mobile banking apps, digital wallets, and fast online payments have made everyday
transactions effortless, but they have also created new openings for cyber criminals.
Telephone fraud, particularly voice phishing (vishing), has become much harder to stop
than spam emails or SMS fraud. Scammers rely on live psychological manipulation—
using urgency, fear of bank account suspension, or fake police threats—to convince
victims to read out OTPs and passwords right during a call.
Most existing defenses still rely on number blacklists or caller-ID databases. These static
systems fail when attackers use spoofed numbers or throwaway VoIP lines. To fix this
gap, this project develops TrueShield AI, an on-device protective system designed to
evaluate what is actually being said during an active conversation. By combining real-
time speech transcription, fast keyword matching, and Large Language Model (LLM)
reasoning, TrueShield AI flags scam behavior while keeping the user's phone call
completely private.
Key Objectives:
• To detect and classify telephone scam attempts in real time as the call happens.
• To perform semantic intent analysis using natural language processing to spot social
engineering tricks.
• To provide an Explainable AI (XAI) alert interface that clearly tells the user why a call
is suspicious.
• To build a bilingual lexicon covering 95+ English, Hindi, and Hinglish fraud phrases
common in Indian cybercrime.
• To keep the application lightweight with low response latency (under 2 seconds) so
it runs smoothly on mobile devices without battery drain.
3.2 Proposed System Architecture
TrueShield AI is built as a modular four-tier pipeline. Instead of running a single heavy
AI model that could slow down the phone, the system divides the workload into
separate, specialized modules: live audio acquisition, streaming speech-to-text
conversion, dual-engine threat analysis, risk score arbitration, and real-time user
notification.
When an incoming call is active on speakerphone with user consent, the audio
monitoring module captures speech chunks in temporary RAM. The speech
recognition engine turns these sound chunks into text segments. From there, the text
is fed into a dual analysis engine: a fast deterministic pattern matcher that flags
known fraud keywords in under 5 milliseconds, and Google Gemini 1.5 Flash, which
examines the deeper conversational intent. Both scores are merged using a
maximum-pooling rule backed by a 6-second watchdog timer, instantly updating the
risk gauge on the user's screen.
25

PDF PAGE 26
Benefits of this Modular Approach:
• Independent Scalability: Each component—from speech transcription to LLM
reasoning—can be upgraded or optimized without breaking the rest of the
application.
• Simplified Maintenance: If a specific module faces network latency or an API
change, it can be debugged in isolation without taking the whole system down.
• Fail-Safe Operation: If cloud-based semantic analysis slows down due to poor
mobile network, the local keyword matcher still protects the user without delay.
• Lower Resource Consumption: Speech audio is processed in small temporary
memory buffers and erased immediately, preventing high RAM usage on the phone.
• Easy Integration: Future defense tools, like deepfake voice detection or synthetic
audio filters, can easily plug into the existing pipeline as an extra layer.

3.3 Audio Monitoring and Speech Acquisition
The Audio Monitoring module is the first entry point of the TrueShield AI pipeline. Its
primary job is to capture incoming telephonic audio in real time, clean up the sound
stream, and deliver continuous audio chunks to the transcription engine. Because
privacy is a core concern, this module works with explicit user consent and keeps all
raw audio in a temporary, circular memory buffer that is deleted as soon as speech is
converted to text.
Core Functions of the Module:
• Real-Time Speech Capture: Captures spoken sound from the device's audio bridge
or speakerphone during an active phone call.
• Buffer Management: Temporarily holds sound in short time-slices (chunks of 200–
500 milliseconds) to prevent audio drops.
• Stream Synchronization: Aligns the audio stream with the downstream speech

3.4 Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
Once audio chunks are captured, the Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) module
turns spoken sound waves into machine-readable text segments. In TrueShield AI,
speech transcription serves as the foundation for all downstream threat checks—if the
transcription misses words, the scam detector cannot evaluate intent accurately.
Sequential Stages in the ASR Pipeline:
• Speech Framing & Windowing: The raw audio stream is divided into short,
overlapping 20-millisecond frames to capture distinct phonetic transitions.
• Acoustic Feature Extraction: The system transforms time-domain speech signals
into frequency-domain feature vectors, primarily using Mel-Frequency Cepstral
Coefficients (MFCCs).
• Acoustic Modeling: Deep neural networks map the extracted acoustic features
against known phonetic probabilities.
• Language Modeling & Decoding: A decoder pairs phonetic outputs with
vocabulary grammar to produce the most probable words and sentences.
• Sliding Buffer Dispatch: As soon as a coherent text phrase accumulates (typically
3–5 words), it is immediately dispatched to the dual-engine analyzer for threat
scoring.

3.5 Text Preprocessing and Cleaning

3.8 Weighted Risk Assessment and Arbitration Engine
TrueShield AI rejects traditional binary classifications like "Spam" or "Not Spam"
because real phone conversations evolve over time. Instead, the Weighted Risk
Assessment engine calculates a dynamic danger score between 0 and 100 as the call
progresses.

PDF PAGE 35
Unlike older systems that rely on a rigid linear weighted sum—which can dangerously
dilute severe threats—TrueShield AI uses a Hybrid Maximum-Pooling Arbitration
Model supported by a watchdog circuit breaker:
Risk Score (R_final) = max( R_lexical , R_semantic )
Where: •
• R_lexical (0–100): The fast deterministic threat score generated locally in
under 5 milliseconds by scanning spoken tokens against the 95+ Hinglish and
English fraud dictionary.
• R_semantic (0–100): The deep contextual intent score generated by Google
Gemini 1.5 Flash after analyzing emotional coercion and impersonation.
• Watchdog Circuit Breaker: A strict 6000-millisecond timer monitors the cloud
LLM branch. If poor cellular connectivity delays the Gemini API response, the
system falls back safely to R_lexical, ensuring threat detection never freezes.
• Immediate Override Rule: If a hard credential harvest marker (such as a direct
demand for OTP, PIN, or CVV) is spotted with a severity of 85 or higher, the
system instantly escalates R_final to the Critical state regardless of
conversational tone.

3.11 Model Evaluation Metrics
To rigorously measure how well TrueShield AI detects fraudulent calls while ignoring
legitimate conversations, the system is evaluated against standard machine learning
and telecommunication security metrics. Because a scam detection tool operates in
real time during active phone calls, evaluation must account for both classification
accuracy and system response speed.
Key Evaluation Metrics and Formulas:
• Accuracy: The proportion of total calls (both scam and legitimate) that were correctly
classified by the pipeline. Formula: Accuracy = (True Positives + True Negatives) / Total
Calls
• Precision: The ratio of correctly flagged scam calls out of all calls that the system
warned the user about. High precision is essential so users do not receive annoying
false alarms during genuine family or business calls. Formula: Precision = True Positives
/ (True Positives + False Positives)
• Recall (Sensitivity): The ability of the system to catch all actual scam attempts without
letting fraudsters slip through undetected. In financial security, recall is critical because
a single missed scam call can result in stolen money. Formula: Recall = True Positives /
(True Positives + False Negatives)
• F1-Score: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced single
score that reflects real-world classification reliability across uneven datasets. Formula:
F1-Score = 2 × (Precision × Recall) / (Precision + Recall)
• End-to-End Response Latency: The total elapsed time from the moment the caller
speaks an audio chunk to the instant a visual alert appears on the user's screen. For
real-time usability, this includes speech framing, transcription, dual-engine analysis,
and risk arbitration.
• False Positive Rate (FPR): The percentage of legitimate phone calls that were
incorrectly flagged as suspicious. The design goal is to keep this number as close to
zero as possible.

5.7 Risk Assessment Modelling
A Weighted Risk Assessment model, for dynamically measuring the probability of
fraud, is adopted in this system. Risk score will be determined from several
parameters, such as:
Risk Assessment Parameters
• Severity of keywords
• Confidence in semantics
• Emotional manipulation
• Number of suspicious words
• Conversation patterns
Risk Score will be calculated as follows:
In TrueShield AI, rather than relying on a rigid linear weighted summation that can
average out severe danger signals, the system utilizes a Hybrid Maximum-Pooling
Arbitration Model with a watchdog circuit breaker:
R_final = max( R_lexical , R_semantic )
Where:
• R_lexical (0–100): Deterministic score generated in under 5 ms by scanning real-
time tokens against the 95+ calibrated Hinglish/English fraud dictionary.
• R_semantic (0–100): Contextual intent score generated by Google Gemini 1.5 Flash
analyzing social engineering coercion, urgency, and authority impersonation.
• Watchdog Circuit Breaker: A strict 6000 ms timeout monitors Branch B. If network
fluctuations delay the cloud LLM, the system fails-safe to R_lexical, ensuring
protection is never dropped.
• Immediate Override Rule: If a hard credential harvest marker (such as asking for
OTP, CVV, or NetBanking PIN) is identified with severity ≥ 85, R_final immediately
escalates to CRITICAL state regardless of conversational tone.
Where:
• (R) – Risk Score
• (K) – Keyword detection score
• (S) – Semantic suspicion score

• (E) – Emotional manipulation score
• (C) – Conversation pattern score

of the key benefits of the presented system is its ability to conduct real-time risk
assessment through the Weighted Risk Assessment approach which allows detecting
the level of risk at any point of time during the conversation and issuing alerts when
users expose personal data to scammers. Another significant advantage of the
presented solution is the possibility of providing Explainable AI functionality and
ensuring transparency.

possibility of creating lightweight scam detection systems based on modern web
solutions. Development of the application using React.js, Node.js, Firebase, Web
