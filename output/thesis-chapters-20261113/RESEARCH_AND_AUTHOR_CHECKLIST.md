# Research and author checklist

## Writing and originality

This is an assisted first draft, not a promise of bypassing a detector. Natural academic writing comes from accurate explanations, specific evidence, the author's judgement and correct attribution—not hidden-character tricks, fabricated citations or repeated rewrites aimed at a score. Review and revise the draft in your own voice and follow your institution's disclosure policy.

Turnitin acknowledges that its writing detector can misidentify text and should not be the sole basis for adverse action: [Using the AI Writing Report](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report). Originality.ai also discusses false positives and contextual human review: [Human-written content flagged as AI](https://help.originality.ai/en/article/human-written-content-flagged-as-ai-what-to-do-next-fa4sd3/). Neither source establishes a reliable way to guarantee an undetectable submission. A similarity score and a prediction of generated authorship measure different things; neither replaces checking attribution and research integrity.

Keep your notes, source annotations, drafts and experiment records. Paraphrase ideas from understanding and cite the original source; retain quotation marks for actual quotations. Do not claim sole unaided authorship where that would contradict your institution's rules.

## Copying into your thesis

The HTML reading copy provides selectable, formatted text. Copy one chapter at a time into your document. Apply Heading 1 to chapter titles and Heading 2 to numbered subsections, then use the editor's automatic table of contents. Use the institution's font, margins, spacing and caption rules. Do not manually preserve the placeholder page numbers in the supplied contents table.

The opening editing notes and this checklist are drafting aids, not thesis body text. Retain the actual limitations within the chapters; remove only the editing instructions after completing them.
